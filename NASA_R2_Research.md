Status: **for team review. No mission data files have been changed.**
Research date: 2026-11-15. Continues [NASA_R1_Research.md](NASA_R1_Research.md) (Opportunity, Apollo Lunar Modules, Pioneer 10). Same method and legend:

- ✅ **Verified**: stated on a NASA page (URL in the source key).
- ⚠️ **Conflict**: two pages disagree. A recommendation is given; the team must confirm.
- ❓ **Not stated**: not found on the pages read. Do not write it into the story until a source is found.

## 1. Challenge-page claims vs NASA mission pages

Source: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/nasas-discarded-equipment-storytelling/ (Details and Resources tabs).

Useful framing from the challenge text: it calls the hardware "left behind… standing, or flying, silently" and says the aim is to explain "what they are, why they were originally sent into space, and the scientific discoveries they made and continue to make". It stresses that hardware "varies widely in both its purpose and current level of functionality", which supports not forcing one timeline on every mission. It lists 15 entry lines that expand to 17 journeys (Spirit/Opportunity, Viking 1/2, Phoenix/InSight and Pioneer 10/11 are pairs).

- ✅ **What it was:** "a set of scientific instruments emplaced at the landing site by the astronauts"; a 25 kg central station supplies power and communications.
- ✅ **Power:** SNAP-27 radioisotope generator, "approximately 70 W DC at 16 V. (63.5 W after one year.)", fuel source "an approximately 4 kg fuel capsule… which contained plutonium-238", generator mass 17 kg (LM12, LM15, LM17 pages). (corrected 2026-11-15, Moon fact-check): this line previously read "about 70 W **at launch**" and "plutonium-238 fuel capsule of about 4 kg"; NASA's text does not say "at launch", and the 4 kg is the capsule's mass, not the amount of plutonium (which NASA does not state).
- ✅ **End:** all ALSEP stations "shut down… on 30 September 1977" (Apollo 12, 14, 15, 16, 17 pages). ❓ The **reason for shutdown is not stated** on any page read; **still unresolved on 2026-11-15** (a human must inspect the scanned PDF; no reason is inferred). (A scanned termination report exists at https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/misc/documents/b32116.pdf but had no readable text.)
- ✅ **Science moment:** the Apollo 12 ascent stage "intentionally crashed into the Moon… about 60 km from the seismic station… creating the first recorded artificial moonquake" (1969-099C).
- ✅ Apollo 14 package had signal and command losses (1975–1977) that were recovered; useful for an honest "it kept working until 1977" story (1971-008C).
- ❓ Whether retroreflectors are still used today, and data-return totals, are **not stated** on pages read. Retroreflector locations are listed in SITES (Apollo 11: 0.67345° N, 23.47307° E; Apollo 14: 3.64417° S, 17.47865° W; Apollo 15: 26.13341° N, 3.62850° E). The challenge page lists "retroreflectors" among ALSEP items; do not state "still in use" without a source.
